INKLOOM AIROUND 1 · THE PLAN
01 · Executive summary

Trust is built in every message.

Inkloom is a proposed brand-governance workspace that helps organizations review content before it is published. It brings brand rules, content checks, plain-language explanations and human approval into one review loop.

The problem

Content production is distributed across employees, agencies, channels and AI writing tools. The people creating a post, email, product page or campaign may not know the newest approved terminology, which claims have evidence, or who must approve a risky message. A static brand guide can be hard to apply consistently at the moment a draft is being written. The result can be confusing language, unsupported promises, outdated facts, inaccessible copy or off-brand creative reaching an audience before anyone catches it.

Our proposed response

Inkloom places a pre-publication review between drafting and publishing. A creator submits content and its context. The system compares it to approved guidelines and source material, flags specific passages, explains the relevant rule and offers an optional revision. The reviewer can request changes, leave a note or approve the content. Automation informs the decision; it does not make the final decision or publish.

Who benefits

Creators get actionable feedback early. Brand stewards spend less time repeating basic corrections. Legal and communications reviewers can focus on higher-risk claims. Leaders gain a clearer picture of recurring guideline gaps.

Where we are today

The current prototype demonstrates browser-based text checks, editable preferred terms, explained findings, reviewer notes, human decisions, local review history and simple trend summaries. It is a foundation—not yet the full platform described in this plan.

Submission integrity. No interviews, surveys or organizational pilot results have been supplied for this plan. Secondary sources below support the problem rationale; primary user research is explicitly planned, not claimed as completed.

Round 1 goal: validate the most valuable review workflow, define a responsible technical path and establish measurable criteria for the next build stage.

INKLOOM AIPROBLEM & EVIDENCE
02 · Problem definition and evidence

Guidelines do not review a draft by themselves.

The governance gap is the distance between a brand rule being documented and that rule being followed in a specific piece of content.

Distributed creation

More contributors and channels create more handoffs. Drafting speed can outpace review capacity.

Scattered authority

Rules, claims, approved facts and assets may live in different documents, drives or people’s memory.

Late correction

Manual review often detects issues near publication, when revisions are expensive or deadlines are tight.

What the evidence supports

  1. Distributed brand work is a recognized operational challenge. Marq’s brand-consistency guidance describes the challenge of enabling many creators and functions to produce content without diluting a brand. This is useful industry context from a brand-management vendor, not independent causal research or a measurement of Inkloom’s target users [1].
  2. Objective advertising claims need substantiation. The U.S. Federal Trade Commission’s advertising-substantiation policy states that advertisers should have a reasonable basis for objective claims before dissemination. This makes claim review a real governance concern, although legal rules vary by jurisdiction and claim type [2].
  3. Accessibility is defined through testable content criteria. W3C’s Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 specify success criteria for accessible web content. Descriptive link purpose and understandable text are relevant considerations; Inkloom’s current text heuristics are not a WCAG conformance audit [3].
Our working problem hypothesis: teams need a quicker, more traceable way to apply approved brand rules during content creation—without replacing subject-matter or legal judgment.

Evidence still needed

The public sources establish that brand consistency, claim substantiation and accessibility are recognized concerns. They do not prove how often these problems occur in a particular organization, how much time they cost, or whether Inkloom is the preferred solution. Those questions are the first research tasks in Round 1.

Sources are listed in full on page 12. No market-size, ROI, error-rate or user-survey figure is asserted in this plan.

INKLOOM AISOLUTION & VALUE
04 · Proposed solution and value proposition

Make the right action clearer than the warning.

Inkloom is a review assistant, not a content gatekeeper. Its central design principle is to make checks understandable, evidence-linked and contestable.

Product promise

For teams creating content at scale, Inkloom brings approved brand guidance into the draft-review moment—surfacing potential inconsistencies with explanations and optional fixes, while keeping approval and publication decisions with authorized people.

How value is created

For creators

Earlier feedback reduces uncertainty. A highlighted phrase plus a plain-language reason is more actionable than “off-brand.” Optional suggestions preserve the creator’s intent.

For reviewers

Structured findings can help focus attention on meaningful concerns instead of repeatedly correcting known terminology issues. Review notes make decisions easier to understand later.

For brand teams

Repeated findings show where training or guidance may be needed. A governed source library can reduce reliance on stale copies and informal memory.

For the organization

A documented, human-controlled process can improve consistency and accountability. Benefits must be tested; this plan does not claim measured savings or reduced risk yet.

Product principles

  • Evidence before certainty: distinguish organization-approved facts from generated suggestions and external references.
  • Explain before edit: state what was flagged and why; never silently rewrite the creator’s content.
  • Human authority: AI may assist, but only authorized people approve or reject publication.
  • Proportionate friction: route uncertain or high-impact issues for review; do not block low-risk work on weak signals.
  • Privacy by design: minimize draft data, control access and make storage and service use visible.

North-star outcome: a reviewer can make a timely, well-informed decision with an understandable record of the content, guidance and rationale involved.

INKLOOM AIEND-TO-END WORKFLOW
05 · Proposed product workflow

From brief to accountable decision.

1

Set up a governed workspace. An administrator configures roles and imports the current brand guide, approved terminology, dated fact sheets, claim evidence and visual-asset rules. Every source has an owner, effective date and version.

2

Submit content with context. A creator selects a format/channel, audience, campaign and intended publication date; then pastes or uploads a draft. They can mark sensitive information and attach a source or desired message.

3

Run checks against approved sources. Deterministic checks handle exact terminology and format rules. Retrieval-based AI may compare context with approved source excerpts. Each finding records its category, excerpt, severity, explanation, source version and uncertainty.

4

Revise with creator control. Inkloom proposes alternatives that preserve intent. The creator applies, edits or dismisses each suggestion and can add context. Original and revised versions remain distinguishable.

5

Route to the right reviewer. Rules based on content type and risk direct the draft to a brand, legal, accessibility or compliance reviewer. Reviewers comment, request changes, escalate uncertainty or approve.

6

Record outcome and learn. The system records who decided, when, what changed and which guideline applied. Aggregated trends help owners update unclear rules—without turning raw individual activity into punitive employee scoring.

Illustrative scenario (not user research)

A marketer submits an email containing “guaranteed savings” and the phrase “click here.” Inkloom flags the comparative promise because its approved evidence library contains no matching substantiation, and suggests descriptive link wording. The marketer can revise or attach evidence. A designated reviewer makes the final decision and records a brief rationale. If the fact source is missing or stale, the system marks the issue uncertain and routes it for human verification.

Workflow guardrail: “No findings” means only that configured checks did not find a match. It is never displayed as a guarantee that a draft is accurate, compliant or ready to publish.

Planned source and integration model

Start with administrator-uploaded brand documents and manually approved facts and terminology. Extract text into attributable source chunks; retain owner, version, effective date, confidentiality label and review date. Later integrations may include Google Drive or SharePoint for controlled source sync, Slack/Teams for reviewer notifications, and a CMS for draft handoff. Each connector requires explicit scopes, tenant controls, revocation and a documented data-processing review.

Proposed check flow

Normalize and classify a draft → apply deterministic rules → retrieve only authorized, relevant source passages → generate candidate findings → attach source citations and uncertainty → validate output format → present to a reviewer. If a source is missing, contradictory or expired, return “needs human verification,” not an invented answer.

External AI transparency: the governance workspace currently sends no draft to an AI provider. The repository contains a legacy AI client for unrelated brand-generation routes. A future AI review service must disclose provider, model, region, retention and training policy before activation.

Data minimization: collect only the draft and metadata needed to review it; avoid sending unrelated personal data; log review decisions separately from unnecessary content copies.

INKLOOM AIAI & EXPLAINABILITY
08 · AI approach and reliability

Use automation as evidence, not authority.

Inkloom should combine deterministic rules for crisp requirements with carefully constrained AI for contextual language—not ask a model to invent organizational facts or act as a compliance decision-maker.

Method by problem type

  • Exact requirements: code-based checks for terminology, required disclaimers, prohibited phrases and character/channel constraints.
  • Contextual suggestions: an AI model may assess tone, likely unsupported claims or message alignment only when given the relevant, approved guideline/source text.
  • Evidence-grounded facts: retrieve a dated approved source, show the supporting excerpt and flag mismatch or lack of evidence. Retrieval is not proof that the source itself is correct or current.
  • Visual and accessibility checks: later use deterministic measurements where appropriate, with assistive-technology and human review; image interpretation alone is insufficient.

Required explanation for each finding

Show

Exact text or asset, issue category, severity rationale, applicable rule, source excerpt/version, suggested action and uncertainty.

Allow

Accept suggestion, edit it, dismiss it with a reason, attach evidence, request clarification or escalate to an authorized reviewer.

Reliability plan

Create a consented, de-identified evaluation set covering channels, writing styles, edge cases, stale facts, conflicting rules and non-English content if supported. Have qualified reviewers label true issues, false alarms and missed issues. Report precision and recall by category, severity and user group; track calibration and reviewer override rates. Use release thresholds defined with users and risk owners, not a single blended “brand score.” Regression-test every rule and prompt change.

Safe fallback: when there is no applicable source, output is malformed, or sources disagree, do not produce a success-shaped verdict. Explain the gap and route it to a human.

Current prototype: deterministic heuristics only. There is no LLM-based governance review, source retrieval, factual verification or reliability benchmark in the current review workspace.

INKLOOM AIRESPONSIBLE DESIGN
09 · Privacy, security, accessibility and human control

Protect the material people trust us with.

Privacy & confidentiality

Private-by-default workspaces; data minimization; clear purpose and consent; configurable retention/deletion; no customer-draft training by default; redact sensitive content before external processing; explain service providers and data locations.

Security & access

Tenant isolation; least-privilege creator/reviewer/admin roles; strong authentication; secure transport and encryption at rest; secret management; rate limits; audit events; backups; dependency and incident-response procedures.

Accessibility & inclusion

Keyboard-operable workflows, semantic headings and labels, visible focus, accessible status announcements, sufficient contrast, plain-language findings, screen-reader testing and representative user testing. Target WCAG 2.2 AA as a design goal, not a current certification.

Human oversight

AI findings are advisory; authorized humans decide. High-impact claims require evidence and review. Users can challenge errors, add context and request source correction. No automated publication or punitive individual scoring.

Failure and misuse scenarios

  • False negative: a harmful or unsupported statement passes. Mitigate with disclaimers, risk-based review, test sets and explicit “not verified” status.
  • False positive: the system disrupts an acceptable creative choice. Provide explanations, dismissal, appeal and rule-owner feedback.
  • Stale or poisoned source: an outdated fact misleads the reviewer. Require provenance, owner, dates, change logs and expiry/escalation.
  • Confidentiality leak: drafts or guidelines reach an unapproved provider or user. Minimize processing, restrict access, audit data flows and support deletion.
  • Automation bias: a high score is treated as clearance. Avoid blanket pass scores and train users that checks are incomplete.
Present-day limitation: the prototype uses localStorage, which is not a secure team repository; drafts remain on the browser until cleared. It has no governance-role authorization. Do not enter confidential production material or rely on it for enterprise review.

Immediate decisions and roadmap

Now: complete primary research, confirm a pilot team and select a small set of approved text guidelines. Next: move from browser-only records to secure team storage with roles, provenance and version history. Then: evaluate source-grounded claim and fact checks. Later: consider document, image, asset and CMS integrations only after demand, security and evaluation quality are demonstrated.

Risks and known limitations

Users may mistake suggestions for verified facts; the prototype can miss issues or over-flag; source material can be stale; and local storage is not suitable for shared confidential work. Integrations and AI introduce privacy, security, cost and latency risks. The MVP must disclose these constraints and maintain a human decision path.
